Privacy Policy

Yahora — a minimalist event logger for Android

Esta política también está disponible en español.

Applies to the Android application Yahora (package com.podxboq.yahora), published by podxboq.
Last updated: 22 August 2026.

Yahora collects no data whatsoever. It has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting and no third-party trackers. It does not even request permission to use the network, so it cannot send anything anywhere — not by design, and not by accident.

What the app stores

Everything you record in Yahora is kept in a private database on your own device, inside the app's own storage area, which the Android system keeps isolated from other applications. That data consists of exactly two things:

  • the names of the tags you create, and whether each one is marked as a favourite;
  • the date and time of each entry you log by tapping a tag.

Nothing else is recorded. Yahora does not read your contacts, your location, your files, your accounts or your device identifiers, and it holds no field in which personal information could be entered other than a tag's name, which you choose yourself.

What is sent off your device

Nothing. The application declares no permissions at all in its manifest — including android.permission.INTERNET — so the operating system itself prevents it from opening a network connection. There is no server behind Yahora, and its developer has no way of seeing what you log.

Android's own backup

Yahora allows Android's standard backup mechanism to include its data, so that your history survives moving to a new phone. If you have device backup switched on in your Android settings, the system may therefore copy the app's database to your Google account, end-to-end encrypted with a key derived from your device credentials. This transfer happens between you, your device and Google, under Google's privacy policy — the developer of Yahora takes no part in it and has no access to the result. You can turn it off in your device's backup settings.

Permissions

None. Yahora requests no runtime permissions and declares none in its manifest.

Third parties

Yahora contains no advertising SDK, no analytics SDK and no third-party service of any kind. The only libraries it is built with are Android's own Jetpack components (Compose, Room and Lifecycle), which run locally and send nothing anywhere.

Children

Yahora is not directed at children in particular, and it is equally safe for them: since it collects nothing from anyone, it collects nothing from children either.

Deleting your data

Because your data never leaves your device, you are the only one who holds it, and removing it needs no request to anybody. Clearing the app's storage (Settings → Apps → Yahora → Storage → Clear data) or uninstalling the app erases everything it has recorded. If you have Android's backup switched on, remember that a copy may remain in your own Google account until you delete it there.

Changes to this policy

Should this policy ever change, the revised version will be published at this same address and its date updated. The page lives in the app's public source repository, so every revision it has ever had is recorded in the project's git history.

Contact
